# DPIA threshold check (Art. 35 GDPR)

*Schwellwertanalyse.* Whether NRIClub needs a data protection impact
assessment (DPIA), written 16 October 2026 and updated 26 October 2026 for
the owner to decide, with the lawyer. If a DPIA is required, a data
protection officer is required too (§ 38(1) sentence 2 BDSG), whatever the
number of people.

## What NRIClub does

A members-only social network for Indians abroad: profiles, a group for
each city, posts, questions (including health and visa topics, and urgent
questions to up to 500 members of a city), events, a Market (rooms, jobs,
items) with a housing search that matches "Looking for a place" posts to
listings, a travel board where members post trips with flight numbers and
dates to find a companion, places (including temples and doctors), private
messages, free mentoring, city ambassadors. Members can turn on a public
preview for a Market listing, event, question or trip, which anyone with
the link sees (questions: the title; not in Health and Legal/Visa). A team
checks every new member by LinkedIn profile or a note. Today a handful of
members; the aim is 10,000 or more.

## The nine criteria (EDPB guidelines WP 248 rev.01)

Two or more criteria usually mean a DPIA is needed.

| # | Criterion | NRIClub | Met? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evaluation or scoring, profiling | No scoring or profiling. Admins judge each sign-up by hand | No |
| 2 | Automated decisions with legal or similar effect | No. The only automatic step holds a sign-up that matches a declined or blocked account until a person decides | No |
| 3 | Systematic monitoring | No third-party tracking. Own website analytics only with consent (privacy banner): pseudonymous visitor ID, no IP address stored (since 26 October 2026), approximate place, single visits with their pages shown to admins with times to the hour, not linked to accounts, kept 6 months; "last active" time. The travel board shows members' flight numbers and dates to other members (members choose to post them; deleted 30 days after the trip) | No (borderline for the single-visit view) |
| 4 | Sensitive data (Art. 9) or highly personal data | Yes: membership, hometown, temple recommendations, festivals, health questions can reveal ethnic origin, religion, health; private messages; flight numbers and travel dates (where a member will be, and when). A question's title in a public preview can reveal them to anyone with the link | **Yes** |
| 5 | Large scale | Not today. At 10,000+ members across many countries it may be | **Later, likely** |
| 6 | Matching or combining datasets | Only the match against the record of declined and blocked accounts | Borderline |
| 7 | Vulnerable data subjects | Adults only. Some members are new arrivals depending on the community (housing, jobs, visas), but not "vulnerable" in the usual sense | Borderline |
| 8 | Innovative technology | No | No |
| 9 | Prevents people from exercising a right or using a service | The membership check and the automatic hold decide access to the service | **Yes** |

Result: at least two criteria (4 and 9), with a third (5) as membership
grows. The public previews add a way for Art. 9 data to leave the
members-only area (by the member's choice, per item; warned against in the
switch; not for Health and Legal/Visa questions), and the travel board
adds data about where members will be: both belong in a DPIA if one is
done.

## The German "must" list (Art. 35(4))

The German supervisory authorities publish a list of processing operations
that always need a DPIA (DSK, "Liste der Verarbeitungstätigkeiten, für die
eine DSFA durchzuführen ist"). **[Check with the lawyer which entries could
apply, in particular large-scale processing of special-category data.]**

## Recommendation (for the owner to decide)

- The lawyer advised doing the DPIA before NRIClub reaches about 1,000
  members (below). Treat it as required, and do it before inviting the
  public beyond the first members, or at the latest by then.
- Then appoint a data protection officer (an external one is fine), publish
  the contact in the Privacy Policy (section 1) and notify the supervisory
  authority (Art. 37(7)).
- If the lawyer concludes that no DPIA is needed, write the reasons here and
  keep calling nobody a data protection officer.

The lawyer's view (October 2026, `questions-for-the-lawyer.md`): besides
§ 38(1) sentence 2 BDSG, Art. 37(1)(c) GDPR itself requires a data
protection officer once the core activity is large-scale processing of
special-category data, which a diaspora network is; treat "large scale" as
reached at a few thousand members across several countries. Do the DPIA
before opening to the public at scale (the 1,000-member mark above is
reasonable).
